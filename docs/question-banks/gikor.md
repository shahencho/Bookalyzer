## 1. Book brief

- **Kind of book:** Tumanyan's short realist story (1895). No magic, no
  rescue, a child protagonist and an ending that kills him.
- **Readers in this app:** about 9–13. Younger children can follow it, but the
  ending needs an adult nearby.
- **Author's aim:** to show what "sending the boy to the city to make
  something of himself" actually cost a peasant family, without a villain and
  without consolation.
- **Big ideas:** **A** poverty, not cruelty, is what kills Gikor — the master
  is ordinary · **B** apprenticeship meant unpaid domestic service · **C**
  Gikor keeps his father's three rules perfectly and it saves him from
  nothing · **D** Hambo never thinks to hold anyone to account, and that
  silence is the story's judgement on the whole arrangement.
- **What children feel:** the homesickness, the beating over the cherries, the
  boy standing in the frost, and the clothes carried home.
- **Cultural context:** essential. This is a set text in Armenian schools and
  the reference point for a whole genre of "village boy sent to the city"
  literature.

**Key scenes:** the mother's tears · the road to the city · the father's
advice · the samovar and the dishes · Dedi's kindness · the cherries and the
beating · the shop door in the frost · the hospital · the clothes taken home.

**Unsure / VERIFY:** the city is not named in the questions (it is understood
to be Tiflis but the text does not insist). The exact illness is not named
either; the bank says only that he catches cold and is taken to hospital.

## 3. Design for this book

- **Why this type mix:** a short linear story suits ordering, and the
  village/city classification makes the imbalance visible — two items on one
  side, four on the other, which is exactly the shape of the text. The
  Evaluate question is deliberately the hardest in the batch ("whose fault?"),
  because the story refuses to answer it. The Create question asks for the
  letter Gikor never wrote, which requires knowing his character rather than
  inventing a happier plot.
- **Coverage map:** the father's advice → q1 (idea C) · the household work →
  q2 (idea B) · the word "apprentice" → q3 (idea B) · the cherries → q4
  (idea A) · the whole arc → q5 · the cast → q6 (idea A) · village vs city →
  q7 · the frost → q8 (idea A) · whose fault → q9 (Evaluate, idea D) · the
  unwritten letter → q10 (Create) · homesickness → q11 · the ending → q12
  (idea D).

## 4. Proposed bank

**Three examples:**

1. *"Why does Gikor's father send him to the city?"* → **"What advice does
   Hambo give his son as they part?"** The old answer opens the summary; the
   new one introduces the three rules that Gikor then keeps to the letter — and
   which do not save him. That irony is the story.
2. *open/Create: "add one new scene showing a happy day for Gikor"* →
   **"Imagine Gikor sent one letter home. What would he write, and what would
   he keep from her?"** The old task invites a child to rewrite the book into
   something kinder. The new one stays inside it, and the "what would he hide"
   half is where the understanding shows.
3. *"What is the most important lesson the story teaches?"* → **"Whose fault
   is what happened — the father's, the master's, or nobody's?"** From a
   recitation to the argument the story actually stages, with a grading brief
   that accepts all three verdicts.
