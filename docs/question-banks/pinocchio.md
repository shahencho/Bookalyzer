## 1. Book brief

- **Kind of book:** Collodi's 1883 novel, serialised in a children's paper and
  far stranger and harder than its reputation. Episodic, moralising, funny and
  genuinely frightening.
- **Readers in this app:** about 8–12.
- **Author's aim:** to show a child what it costs other people when you do
  exactly as you please — and to make the transformation at the end something
  paid for rather than granted.
- **Big ideas:** **A** every gift Pinocchio is given is lost inside a chapter,
  always by his own choice · **B** Geppetto keeps giving up things he needs ·
  **C** the traps work because the victim wants them to be true · **D** the
  punishment in Collodi is literal — boys who live like animals become animals.
- **What children love:** the talking log, the nose, the money tree, the Land
  of Toys, the donkey's ears, and the inside of the shark.

**Key scenes:** the log that talks before it is carved · the hammer and the
Talking Cricket (ch. 4) · the feet burned off on the brazier · Geppetto's coat
sold for the schoolbook · the schoolbook sold for a puppet show · Fire Eater's
five gold pieces · the Field of Wonders · hanged from the oak · the girl with
blue hair and the growing nose · five months in the Land of Toys · the donkey ·
the Terrible Shark · the water wheel and the real boy.

**Unsure / VERIFY:** all keys were grepped in Gutenberg ebook 500 — the hammer,
the brazier, the coat, the five gold pieces, the Field of Wonders, the five
months, the ears and braying, the "Terrible Shark" (**not** a whale), the two
years Geppetto spends inside it, and the closing work for the sick Fairy.

## 3. Design for this book

- **Why this type mix:** the film test drove everything. Three questions are
  spent on scenes the adaptations remove outright (hammer, brazier, coat), one
  fillblank exists purely to correct the whale, and the classification turns the
  Land of Toys advertisement against itself so the child has to hold promise and
  price side by side. The Evaluate question is deliberately one the book itself
  leaves open.
- **Coverage map:** the Cricket → q1 (A, B) · the feet → q2 (A) · the coat →
  q3 (B) · the Fox and Cat → q4 (C) · first six chapters → q5 (A) · four
  characters → q6 (B) · promise vs price → q7 (C, D) · the donkeys → q8 (D) ·
  the unpunished killing → q9 (Evaluate) · invent a trap → q10 (Create, C) ·
  the shark, not the whale → q11 · what earns the ending → q12.

## 4. Proposed bank

**Three examples:**

1. *"Every time Pinocchio tells a lie, his ___ grows longer."* → **"A Talking
   Cricket warns him that boys who refuse to work come to a bad end. What does
   Pinocchio do?"** The nose is the one fact a child has before reading. The
   hammer is the fact that tells them what book this actually is.
2. *"What happens to the boys on Pleasure Island?"* → a classification of six
   items: three things the boys are **promised**, three things that **happen**.
   Same scene, but now the child has to see the swap Collodi is making.
3. *open: "Did Pinocchio deserve to become a real boy?"* → **"He kills the
   Cricket and is never punished for it. Should the book have punished him?"**
   Both positions score full marks; neither can be argued without the book.
