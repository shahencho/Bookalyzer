## 1. Book brief

- **Kind of book:** episodic comic fantasy (1955) set in an ordinary Stockholm
  flat with one impossible element in it. Short chapters, heavy on dialogue.
- **Readers in this app:** about 6–10.
- **Author's aim:** to give a lonely, well-behaved child a friend who is his
  exact opposite — and to let that friend be genuinely badly behaved rather
  than secretly wise.
- **Big ideas:** **A** Karlsson is what Kid would be if nobody could tell him
  off · **B** the pattern never varies: boast, disaster, "a trifle", escape ·
  **C** Kid is lonely in a perfectly nice family · **D** the adults do not
  believe in Karlsson, which makes the friendship entirely Kid's own.
- **What children love:** the button and the propeller, the boasting, the
  steam engine, and the fact that Karlsson never, ever apologises.

**Key scenes:** the first buzzing at the window · the house behind the
chimney · the steam engine · the buns · "the world's best" at everything ·
the trifle · the adults' disbelief · the birthday and Bimbo.

**Unsure / VERIFY:** the catchphrases and the steam-engine episode are
universally quoted but could not be confirmed in the source I checked, so they
appear as *general* patterns rather than as keys tied to a specific chapter.
Miss Bock and Uncle Julius belong to the later volumes and are not used at
all. Bosse, Bettan, Bimbo, Svante Svantesson, the propeller, the button and
the house behind the chimney were verified.

## 3. Design for this book

- **Why this type mix:** as with Emil, an episodic book cannot be ordered by
  plot, so the ordering question sequences the *repeating shape* of a visit —
  six stages, and the last two in the revealing order (Karlsson leaves, then
  Kid is blamed). Classification puts Kid and Karlsson side by side so the
  child can see that Karlsson is a wish. The Create task asks for all three
  beats of a Karlsson joke.
- **Coverage map:** the propeller → q1 · the house → q2 · "in his prime" → q3
  · "a trifle" → q4 (idea B) · the visit pattern → q5 (idea B) · the family →
  q6 (idea C) · Kid vs Karlsson → q7 (idea A) · why Kid forgives him → q8
  (idea C) · good friend or bad → q9 (Evaluate) · invent a boast → q10
  (Create) · the dog → q11 · the adults' disbelief → q12 (idea D).

## 4. Proposed bank

**Three examples:**

1. *fillblank "Karlsson's little house is hidden on the ___"* → **an MC with
   four plausible hiding places.** The old version could be answered from the
   book's title; the new one needs the detail that it is behind a chimney.
2. *open: "Why do you think Kid needed a friend like Karlsson?"* → **"Is
   Karlsson a good friend or a bad one?"** The old question has one acceptable
   answer and asks the child to agree with the book. The new one is a real
   argument, and the grading brief says the best answers hold both sides at
   once.
3. *"If Karlsson had to pick one moment to prove he is the world's best, which
   would it be?"* → **"Invent a new boast: what would he claim, what would he
   do, and how would it go wrong?"** The old question has no defensible
   answer; the new one tests whether the child has noticed the three-beat joke.
