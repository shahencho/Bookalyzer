## 1. Book brief

- **Kind of book:** a long episodic fantasy (1906–07) that is also, literally,
  a school textbook: it was commissioned as a geography reader for Swedish
  schools.
- **Readers in this app:** about 9–13. Long, and easier to read in parts.
- **Author's aim:** to teach the geography of a country by flying a child over
  all of it, and to hang a moral transformation on the journey.
- **Big ideas:** **A** the punishment fits the crime exactly — the boy who
  tormented small creatures becomes one · **B** he can hear what the animals
  think of him before he ever leaves the farm · **C** the route is the
  syllabus; the book's shape comes from its purpose · **D** the way back is
  not a task to complete but a person to become.
- **What children love:** the tomte, the leap onto the gander's neck, Akka,
  and Smirre following them for a year.

**Key scenes:** the farm and the mischief · the tomte in the net and the
refused coin · the shrinking · Martin trying to fly · the grab at the neck ·
Akka's suspicion · Smirre · the provinces one by one · the condition for
becoming human.

**Unsure / VERIFY:** the Glimminge Castle rats-and-pipe episode and the reason
Nils fails to save the sunken city are **not** used — I could not confirm the
details. The commissioning of the book, the tomte, the coin, Martin, Akka,
Smirre and the condition for the change were all verified.

## 3. Design for this book

- **Why this type mix:** the book's origin as a school reader gives two of the
  best questions in the bank (q1 and q8) and the Create task, which asks the
  child to do what Lagerlöf did for their own home. Ordering is deliberately
  confined to the first morning, because the rest of the book is episodic and
  has no fixed sequence a reader could reconstruct. The classification tracks
  the transformation, which is the only through-line.
- **Coverage map:** the commission → q1 (idea C) · the tomte and the coin →
  q2 (idea A) · Martin → q3 · Akka → q4 · the first morning → q5 (idea B) ·
  four characters → q6 · before and during → q7 (idea D) · why the whole
  country → q8 (idea C) · was the punishment fair → q9 (Evaluate, idea A) ·
  your own place from the air → q10 (Create, idea C) · the size → q11 · the
  condition → q12 (idea D).

## 4. Proposed bank

**Three examples:**

1. *"What kind of boy is Nils at the start?"* → **"What was this book
   originally commissioned to be?"** The old answer is the summary's first
   line. The new one is the fact that explains why the book is shaped the way
   it is — and children find it genuinely surprising.
2. *"Why don't the geese trust Nils?"* → **"Sort each detail: the Nils of the
   first chapter, or the Nils of the journey."** The old question can be
   answered without reading; the new one requires evidence from both ends of
   the book.
3. *open: "What did Nils learn on his journey?"* → **"Imagine the geese
   carried him over where you live: what would he see from above, and what
   could he only learn by landing?"** From a slogan to a task that reproduces
   the author's own method.
