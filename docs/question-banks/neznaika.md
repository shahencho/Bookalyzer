## 1. Book brief

- **Kind of book:** Soviet comic fairy tale (1954), first of a trilogy. Short
  chapters, a large cast, one running joke that holds the whole world together.
- **Readers in this app:** about 6–10.
- **Author's aim:** to build a town where a person's name *is* their skill, and
  then follow the one inhabitant whose name says he has none.
- **Big ideas:** **A** every name is a job, which makes Neznaika's name a
  verdict · **B** he abandons music, art and poetry the moment each stops
  being easy · **C** he lies about the balloon not for gain but to be looked
  at · **D** he is forgiven, and only then does he start to learn.
- **What children love:** the cucumber-sized world, the names, the balloon,
  and the moment Znayka walks in.
- **Cultural context:** for Russian-speaking children Neznaika is as familiar
  as Buratino; the names are common nouns in conversation.

**Key scenes:** the town and the names · the parrot clothes · the failed
musician, artist and poet · the balloon built by the whole street · the
flight and the cooling air · the landing in the Green City · Sineglazka ·
Medunitsa's hospital · Znayka's arrival · the farewell ball and the journey
home.

**Unsure / VERIFY:** Neznaika's specific choice of instrument and the text of
his verses are famous but were not confirmed in the source I used; they appear
in the summary as a general pattern and are not the answer to any question.

## 3. Design for this book

- **Why this type mix:** the naming rule is the book's spine, so it appears
  three times in different forms: as an Understand question about Neznaika's
  own name, as the matching question, and as the Create task. The balloon
  carries the ordering question, and the classification contrasts the two
  towns, which is how Nosov structures the second half.
- **Coverage map:** the scale → q1 · the name → q2 (idea A) · the parrot
  clothes → q3 (idea C) · whose idea → q4 · the journey → q5 · the four names
  → q6 (idea A) · two towns → q7 · why he lies → q8 (idea C) · should he have
  been forgiven → q9 (Evaluate, idea D) · invent a shorty → q10 (Create,
  idea A) · Sineglazka → q11 · homesickness → q12.

## 4. Proposed bank

**Three examples:**

1. *"Where do the shorties live?"* → **"How big are the shorties?"** The town
   name is in the summary; the scale is the thing that makes the world work,
   and the explanation connects it to the Cucumber River and the
   watermelon-sized balloon.
2. *open: "Is it good to pretend you know things you don't?"* → **"Was it
   right to forgive him, or should there have been a punishment?"** The old
   question has one permitted answer. The new one is a real disagreement, and
   the grading brief scores both.
3. *open/Create (generic)* → **"Invent a new shorty: what is their name, what
   do they do, and how does the name fit the work?"** This is the one task
   that cannot be done without understanding the joke the entire book is built
   on.
