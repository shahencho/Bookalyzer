## 1. Book brief

- **Kind of book:** Dahl's 1972 sequel, two adventures joined in the middle —
  a space farce and a story about age. Short chapters, very fast.
- **Readers in this app:** about 7–11.
- **Author's aim:** to take two ideas — a lift that can go anywhere, a pill
  that changes your age — and follow each one past the point of safety.
- **Big ideas:** **A** an invention is only as safe as the least careful
  person who gets hold of it · **B** wanting to be young again is a wish
  people make without thinking it through · **C** adults in authority panic
  and posture; a child and one eccentric solve the problem · **D** Dahl obeys
  his own invented rules exactly, which is what makes the nonsense work.
- **What children love:** the Knids, the towing rope, the babies in the bed,
  Minusland, and Grandma Georgina at 358.

**Key scenes:** the launch · panic in Washington · docking with the Space
Hotel · the Knids forming letters · the tow home and the burn-up ·
Wonka-Vite · the babies · Georgina at minus two · Minusland · Vita-Wonk ·
the letter from the President.

**Unsure / VERIFY:** the Knids spell out a word made of their own bodies, but
I did not verify the exact wording in a source, so no key depends on it — the
matching option says only "including letters". The President's staff names
(the nanny, the cat) are likewise unused.

## 3. Design for this book

- **Why this type mix:** the book has an unusually clean two-part structure,
  so classification splits on it. The four inventions make a natural matching
  set. Two questions target the arithmetic, because that is the one thing a
  child who merely watched the story would not retain. The Create task asks
  for a *pair* of opposite sweets, mirroring Dahl's own construction.
- **Coverage map:** the Space Hotel opening → q1 (idea C) · re-entry → q2 ·
  twenty years → q3 (idea D) · the babies → q4 (idea B) · both halves → q5 ·
  the four inventions → q6 · space vs factory → q7 · minus two → q8 (idea D) ·
  should he keep inventing → q9 (idea A) · invent a pair → q10 · Vita-Wonk →
  q11 · the President's letter → q12.

## 4. Proposed bank

**Three examples:**

1. *"What are the shape-shifting alien creatures called?"* → **"Why can the
   Knids not follow the elevator down to Earth?"** The name is in the summary;
   the reason is a real piece of the book (and real physics).
2. *"What quality helps Charlie stay calm during the danger?"* → **"Why does
   Grandma Georgina disappear instead of just becoming young?"** A virtue
   question replaced by the arithmetic the plot turns on.
3. *fillblank "the candy that makes a person younger is called ___"* → **"each
   pill takes ___ years off"**. The old answer is guessable from the book's
   cover copy; the new one requires having read the dosage scene, and it makes
   the child's own sum work.
