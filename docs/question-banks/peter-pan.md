## 1. Book brief

- **Kind of book:** the 1911 novel *Peter and Wendy*, not the play and not the
  film. Barrie's narrator is arch, intrusive and often sad, and the book is
  harsher than its reputation.
- **Readers in this app:** about 8–12.
- **Author's aim:** to write about a child who cannot grow up as a loss rather
  than a wish — and to let the reader feel it only at the very end.
- **Big ideas:** **A** the nursery is already half enchanted before Peter
  arrives · **B** Neverland is genuinely dangerous, and Wendy is nearly killed
  on her first day · **C** the minor characters do all the real harm and all
  the real saving · **D** Peter forgets, and by the last page that is the
  saddest thing in the book.
- **What children love:** the shadow, the flying directions, Tinker Bell, the
  crocodile with the clock, and the clapping.

**Key scenes:** the shadow in the drawer · the sewing · the flying lesson ·
"second to the right" · Tootles' arrow · the little house · the mermaids'
lagoon · Hook and Smee · the poisoned medicine · the clapping · the pirate
ship · the window · Jane and Margaret.

**Unsure / VERIFY:** none. The directions, Tootles' arrow, the poisoned cup,
the clapping, Hook's arm and the crocodile, Smee, and the Jane/Margaret ending
were all verified in Gutenberg ebook 16.

## 3. Design for this book

- **Why this type mix:** the film test drives the design. Tootles' arrow, the
  shadow-as-errand and the Jane/Margaret ending are all book-specific, and
  three questions are spent on them. The classification is built to make the
  child notice that the nursery is already odd before any magic arrives, which
  is Barrie's real trick. The Create task requires the danger, because
  Neverland without danger is not Neverland.
- **Coverage map:** the shadow → q1 (idea A) · the directions → q2 · the arrow
  → q3 (idea B) · the crocodile's clock → q4 · the first evening → q5 ·
  four minor characters → q6 (idea C) · two worlds → q7 (idea A) · the
  clapping → q8 · Wendy's choice → q9 (Evaluate) · your own Neverland → q10
  (Create, idea B) · Smee → q11 · the ending → q12 (idea D).

## 4. Proposed bank

**Three examples:**

1. *"Who is Peter Pan?"* → **"Why does Peter come to the nursery in the first
   place?"** The old question needs no book at all. The answer to the new one —
   he came back for his shadow — is the kind of small, odd, exact detail the
   novel is full of and the adaptations drop.
2. *"What does the crocodile do?"* → **"Why can Hook always hear it coming?"**
   Same creature, but the new version reaches the clock, and the explanation
   can then say what the clock is actually about.
3. *open: "Would you like to go to Neverland?"* → **"Describe your own
   Neverland: two things in it, and one thing that is dangerous."** The old
   question is a preference. The new one has a rule attached that only a
   reader would know to obey.
