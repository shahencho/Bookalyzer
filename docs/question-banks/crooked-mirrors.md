## 1. Book brief

- **Kind of book:** Soviet fairy-tale novella (1951), short chapters, an
  adventure with a very visible moral engine.
- **Readers in this app:** about 8–12.
- **Author's aim:** two lessons at once — a private one (look at yourself from
  the outside) and a public one (a state can be run on lies, and the lies have
  owners).
- **Big ideas:** **A** you see your own faults most clearly in someone else ·
  **B** the mirrors are propaganda: they show the hungry a full plate ·
  **C** the people who own the lie own the country (mirrors and rice — the
  seeing and the eating) · **D** the tyrant's power is entirely other people's
  fear, and ends when one girl refuses to look away.
- **What children love:** the reversed names, the disguise as pages, the two
  keys, and Nushrok stepping backwards off the tower.
- **Cultural context:** for Russian-speaking children this is a classroom
  staple and the 1963 film is famous; the reversed names are a shared joke.

**Key scenes:** the quarrel and the grandmother's wish · the mirror speaks ·
meeting Yalo · the crooked mirror shown to Gurd · the smashed mirror and the
sentence · the palace and the disguise · the key above the throne · losing it ·
Abazh and the second key · the tower roof · Nushrok's fall · "I looked at
myself from the outside".

**Unsure / VERIFY:** the king's reversed name (Yagupop) and a few minor
courtiers are not used in any question, as I could not confirm them in the
sources consulted. The four names that do appear — Nushrok, Abazh, Anidag,
Gurd — are all confirmed, along with what each word spells backwards.

## 3. Design for this book

- **Why this type mix:** a book explicitly about lying vs seeing is made for
  the classification question, and here the two columns are the same kingdom
  twice. The two-key chase gives ordering real work to do. One Analyze question
  is reserved for Nushrok's death, because *how* he dies is the book's whole
  argument about fear. The reversed-name rule becomes a fillblank that works in
  all three languages via Gurd → drug → "friend".
- **Coverage map:** the grandmother's wish → q1 (idea A) · the mirror shown to
  Gurd → q2 (idea B) · reversed names → q3 · the smashed mirror → q4 (idea B) ·
  the two keys → q5 · the four characters → q6 (idea C) · mirrors vs reality →
  q7 (idea B) · Nushrok's fall → q8 (idea D) · did Yalo help Olya → q9
  (Evaluate, idea A) · invent a crooked mirror → q10 (Create, idea C) · the
  Tower of Death → q11 · the closing line → q12 (idea A).

## 4. Proposed bank

**Three examples:**

1. *"Who is Yalo?"* → **"What does the crooked mirror show the starving boy
   Gurd?"** The old answer is the summary's third sentence. The new one is the
   single image the whole political idea rests on.
2. *open: "Why is it wrong to lie?"* → **"Invent a crooked mirror for our own
   world: where would it hang, what lie would it tell, and who would
   benefit?"** The old question needs no book and has one acceptable answer.
   The new one cannot be answered without understanding what Nushrok uses the
   mirrors *for*, and the third part is what tests it.
3. *"Why is the kingdom unfair?"* → **"What makes Nushrok step backwards off
   the tower?"** From a slogan to the most important five seconds in the book:
   nobody pushes him, and a girl who was a coward in chapter one simply does
   not drop her eyes.
