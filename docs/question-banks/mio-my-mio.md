## 1. Book brief

- **Kind of book:** short fantasy novel (1954) with a fairy-tale voice and a
  realistic, painful frame. Much darker in tone than Lindgren's comic books.
- **Readers in this app:** about 8–12.
- **Author's aim:** to write about an unwanted child, and to give him
  everything he lacks in a form the reader is never allowed to verify.
- **Big ideas:** **A** Farawayland supplies exactly what Bosse is missing,
  item for item · **B** the villain is frightening because there is nothing
  inside him to reach · **C** the two small kindnesses of a shopkeeper set
  everything in motion · **D** the book refuses to say whether any of it is
  real.
- **What children love:** the golden apple, the spirit in the bottle, the
  horse with the golden mane, and the words "Mio, my Mio".

**Key scenes:** the park bench · Mrs. Lundin's apple and card · the apple
turning gold · the spirit in the bottle · the king · Miramis and Jum-Jum · the
whispering about the land beyond · setting out · Sir Kato · the last page.

**Unsure / VERIFY:** the mechanics of Kato's defeat and the sword were **not**
verified and are not used in any question; the bank stops at "they set out".
The friend's name appears as Jum-Jum in the Swedish and British texts and as
Pompoo in some American editions — the en file uses Jum-Jum and the summary
does not mention the alternative.

## 3. Design for this book

- **Why this type mix:** the classification is the heart of this bank: six
  items in three matched pairs, so the child discovers for themselves that
  Farawayland is built out of Bosse's exact lacks. The ordering question
  slows down the four steps of the transformation, which most readers
  remember as one blur. The Evaluate question puts the ambiguity to the child
  directly and asks the harder follow-up: does it matter?
- **Coverage map:** Mrs. Lundin → q1 (idea C) · the card → q2 · the name Mio →
  q3 · Miramis → q4 · the transformation → q5 · the four figures → q6
  (idea A) · the two lives → q7 (idea A) · the stone heart → q8 (idea B) ·
  real or not → q9 (Evaluate, idea D) · invent a place → q10 (Create) ·
  "keep out of the way" → q11 · the land beyond → q12.

## 4. Proposed bank

**Three examples:**

1. *"How does Bosse get to Farawayland?"* → **"What does the card he is asked
   to post actually say?"** The old question's answer is in the summary. The
   new one catches the best moment in the book: he reads his own identity
   several minutes before he understands it.
2. *"What is Sir Kato like?"* (options: cruel, frightening, powerful…) →
   **"What is his heart made of?"** From a list of adjectives to the single
   repeated image the whole villain is built on.
3. *open: "Would you like to go to Farawayland?"* → **"Is Farawayland really
   happening, or is Bosse still on the bench? And does your answer change
   anything?"** The old question needs no book. The new one is the question
   the book itself declines to answer, and the second half is what makes it
   hard.
