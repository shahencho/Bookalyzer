## 1. Book brief

- **Kind of book:** Lindgren's 1945 novel, eleven near-independent chapters.
  Comic, anarchic, and much more precisely built than it looks.
- **Readers in this app:** about 7–11 — the youngest book in this batch.
- **Author's aim:** to let one child do exactly as she likes for the length of a
  book, without ever arguing that children should.
- **Big ideas:** **A** Pippi's strength is a plain fact, not a joke, and
  Lindgren keeps proving it · **B** she never wins an argument by arguing —
  she is friendly and immovable · **C** the money and the strength are only ever
  spent on other people · **D** Tommy and Annika's ordinary life is not written
  as a worse life, which is why the book works.
- **What children love:** the horse on the veranda, the feet on the pillow,
  the policemen on the roof, the one morning at school, the circus, the dancing
  burglars, and the birthday where she gives the presents away.

**Key scenes:** moving in with Mr. Nilsson and the horse · Thing-Finding ·
tag with the two policemen and a cookie each · one morning at school and the
horse drawn on the floor · the hollow tree · the bull on the picnic · the
circus strongman · Bloom and Thunder-Karlsson made to dance · the coffee party
and the cream cake · the plank into the burning house · her own birthday party.

**Unsure / VERIFY:** the chapter order and the burglars' names were checked
against the published contents list (see Sources below). The book is **in
copyright**, so nothing could be grepped from a full text; every question was
written from the chapter list plus recall, and no question depends on an exact
quotation. The circus strongman is deliberately not named — the name differs
between translations — and the Thing-Finder chapter is referenced by what she
does rather than by the translated term.

child who had only seen a cartoon poster could have scored full marks.

## 3. Design for this book

- **Why this type mix:** six of the thirteen questions name a specific chapter,
  because the previous bank named none. The ordering question is the real
  sequence of the book rather than an invented "typical day", which makes it
  reading comprehension instead of guesswork. The classification deliberately
  frames Tommy and Annika's household neutrally (idea D) — the child sorts two
  ways of living, not a good one and a bad one. Both duplicate matching and
  classification questions were dropped and the space spent on events.
- **Coverage map:** the horse on the veranda → q1 (A) · the policemen → q2 (B) ·
  the feet on the pillow → q3 · school → q4 · the chapter order → q5 · the
  household → q6 · two houses → q7 (D) · the circus → q8 (A) · the burglars →
  q9 (C) · the gold → q10 (C) · a new grown-up → q11 (Apply, B) · living with
  no grown-ups → q12 (Evaluate) · a Thing-Finding morning → q13 (Create).

## 4. Proposed bank

**Three examples:**

1. *"Who does Pippi live with at Villa Villekulla?"* → **"Pippi owns a horse.
   Where does she keep it?"** The old question is answered by the first sentence
   of the summary. The new one has a real answer — the veranda — and the
   explanation can then say why that detail matters.
2. *"Sort each behaviour into the right group."* → a six-item sort between
   **life next door at Tommy and Annika's** and **life at Villa Villekulla**,
   using the cookies rolled out on the floor and the scrubbing brushes tied to
   her feet. The old version had no book in it at all; this one cannot be done
   without having read the chapters.
3. *open: "What's one everyday task YOU could turn into a game?"* → **"Invent
   one Thing-Finding morning for Pippi: where she looks, what she finds, and
   what she decides to do with it."** Same creative energy, but now the child
   has to imitate something specific — and the grading brief says a find that
   is genuinely valuable has missed the joke.

## 6. Questions for Shahen

- The Russian file keeps Lungina's вилла «Курица» for Villa Villekulla, since
  that is what a Russian-reading child will have in their edition, while the
  Armenian file keeps Վիլլա Վիլլեկուլլա. That inconsistency is deliberate — say
  the word if you would rather all three matched.
- Chapter two of the original contains a racial term for Captain Longstocking's
  kingdom that modern Swedish editions have removed. The bank never touches it:
  the father appears once, in the matching, as "lost overboard in a storm".
