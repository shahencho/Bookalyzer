## 1. Book brief

- **Kind of book:** short modern horror novella for children (2002). Quiet,
  domestic, genuinely frightening. Everything terrible is made of ordinary
  household things.
- **Readers in this app:** about 9–13. Sensitive younger readers find it
  scary; that is the book working as intended.
- **Author's aim:** to write a story in which a bored child is offered
  everything she says she wants, and has to work out why she should refuse it.
- **Big ideas:** **A** being brave means being frightened and doing it anyway ·
  **B** a world built to please you exactly is a trap, because it stops where
  your attention stops · **C** the people who love you imperfectly are the
  real ones · **D** Coraline wins by thinking, planning and keeping her word,
  not by fighting.
- **What children love:** the buttons, the corridor, the cat, the game, the
  hand, and the picnic over the well.

**Key scenes:** the bricked-up door · the tea leaves and the stone · the
corridor · the button offer · the parents gone · the mirror · the three ghost
children · the wager · the search with the stone · the cat thrown, the escape ·
the well.

**Unsure / VERIFY:** the three hiding places of the souls are not used in any
question, because I could not confirm all three from a source. The old man
upstairs is referred to by description rather than by name.

## 3. Design for this book

- **Why this type mix:** the novel is built on a systematic contrast between
  two versions of one world, which is a perfect classification question with
  no ambiguity at all. The chronology matters unusually much (the danger comes
  *after* the escape), so ordering earns its place. Two Analyze questions cover
  the wager and the mist — the two things that explain how the Other Mother
  works and how she can be beaten.
- **Coverage map:** the brick wall → q1 · the buttons → q2 (idea B) · the
  stone → q3 · the ghost children → q4 · the three crossings → q5 · the
  neighbours and the cat → q6 · two worlds → q7 (idea B) · the wager → q8
  (idea D) · would you have stayed → q9 (Evaluate, idea C) · invent an Other
  room → q10 (Create) · the snow globe → q11 · the well → q12 (idea A, D).

## 4. Proposed bank

**Three examples:**

1. *"What is unusual about the Other Mother's and Other Father's eyes?"* →
   **"What always gives away the people in the Other world?"** Same fact, but
   the old version quotes the summary and the new one makes the child think
   about what the buttons *mean* (the price of staying).
2. *"What quality does Coraline rely on most to defeat the Other Mother?"* →
   **"Why does the Other Mother accept a bet she could refuse?"** The old
   question wanted the word "bravery". The new one asks the child to see what
   Coraline saw: that vanity is the creature's only weakness.
3. *open/Create: "imagine one more strange door…"* → **"invent an Other
   version of a room you know: what is better about it, and what one detail is
   wrong?"** The old task could be answered by anyone who likes doors. The new
   one only works if the child has understood that the Other world is both
   attractive and subtly incomplete.
