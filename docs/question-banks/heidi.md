## 1. Book brief

- **Kind of book:** Swiss novel (1881) in two parts, written plainly, with a
  strong contrast between mountain and city and a religious current that
  modern editions usually keep.
- **Readers in this app:** about 7–11.
- **Author's aim:** to argue, through one child, that health and happiness are
  a matter of where and how you live — and to take a child's homesickness
  seriously as an illness.
- **Big ideas:** **A** kindness is not the same as understanding what someone
  needs (the Sesemanns are generous and Heidi is wretched) · **B** Heidi says
  nothing and her body says it for her · **C** she gives without being asked —
  the rolls, the reading to the grandmother · **D** the worst thing Peter does
  turns out to be what saves Clara.
- **What children love:** the hayloft, the goats by name, the melting cheese,
  the hidden rolls, the ghost, and the wheelchair going over the edge.

**Key scenes:** the climb with Dete · the bed of hay and the round window ·
the goats · the blind grandmother · Frankfurt and Fräulein Rottenmeier ·
learning to read · the hoarded rolls · the sleepwalking and the doctor · home
again · Clara's visit · the chair down the mountain · Clara's first steps.

**Unsure / VERIFY:** the names of the servants and the doctor vary between
translations, so no question uses them. The goats Little Swan and Little Bear
and the round window were verified in the Gutenberg text (ebook 1448), as were
the white rolls, the sleepwalking and the wheelchair.

## 3. Design for this book

- **Why this type mix:** the book is built on one contrast, so classification
  carries it with six concrete details rather than the abstract "mountain good,
  city bad". Two questions are spent on the rolls and the sleepwalking, the
  two places where Heidi's silence and her generosity show at once. The
  Evaluate question is about Dete, the only genuinely arguable decision in the
  book.
- **Coverage map:** the hayloft → q1 · the rolls → q2 (idea C) · the diagnosis
  → q3 (idea B) · the ghost → q4 (idea B) · the arc → q5 · four people → q6
  (idea A) · mountain vs Frankfurt → q7 (idea A) · Peter and the chair → q8
  (idea D) · Dete's decision → q9 (Evaluate) · what would you save → q10
  (Create, idea C) · the goats → q11 · Clara walking → q12 (idea D).

## 4. Proposed bank

**Three examples:**

1. *"Who does Heidi go to live with?"* → **"Where does Heidi sleep on her
   first night?"** From the summary's first clause to the hayloft and the
   round window — which the explanation then ties to the Frankfurt windows
   that show nothing but a wall.
2. *"Why is Heidi unhappy in Frankfurt?"* → **"For several nights the house is
   convinced it is haunted. What is really happening?"** The old question can
   be answered by anyone; the new one needs chapter twelve, and it makes the
   same point far more sharply.
3. *open: "Why do you think Heidi misses the mountains so much?"* → **"What
   small thing would you save for one person you know, and why that thing?"**
   The old question asks a child to restate the plot as a feeling. The new one
   asks them to do what Heidi did, and the "why that thing" is what tests
   whether they understood the rolls.
