## 1. Book brief

- **Kind of book:** the third branch of the oral epic *Sasna Tsrer*, sung and
  recited rather than written, in many variants. Episodic and full of
  outsized, funny, violent detail.
- **Readers in this app:** about 9–14.
- **Author's aim:** there is no single author. The branch celebrates a
  defender who wins by refusing to cheat, and who is finally killed by
  treachery rather than in battle.
- **Big ideas:** **A** the enemy is family — the conflict begins in one
  household · **B** David inherits his father's strength and weapons but
  rejects his father's habits (the fenced hunting ground) · **C** defending a
  place means rebuilding it, not only fighting for it · **D** he wins by
  fairness and dies by treachery.
- **What children love:** the talking horse, the lightning sword, the boy who
  cannot control his own strength, the tax collectors thrown out, and the
  single combat.
- **Cultural context:** essential. This is Armenia's national epic, on
  UNESCO's intangible-heritage list; the statue in Yerevan shows David on
  Kurkik Jalali. Getting the enemy wrong is not a small slip here.

**Key scenes:** the baby sent to Msr · the return to Ohan · David the shepherd
and hunter · the fenced hunting ground torn open · the monastery of Marutha
rebuilt · Kouz-Badin and the tax collectors · the sacked monastery · the old
man's proposal · the duel and the three blows · Khandut and Tabriz · Mher the
Younger · the poisoned arrow.

**Unsure / VERIFY:** variants differ on Melik's hiding place in the duel (some
retellings have him in a pit under millstones). The verified account —
three blows first, then one from David — is what the bank uses. Verg/Vergo's
role varies between versions and is not used. No question names the Sultana.

## 3. Design for this book

- **Why this type mix:** an episodic epic with a strong chronology suits
  ordering and a before/after classification pinned to the duel. Matching
  carries the cast, including the horse, which in this epic is a character.
  The single Analyze MC is spent on the duel's rules, because the rules are
  the moral content. The Evaluate question asks about those same rules from
  the other side — was the fairness wise?
- **Coverage map:** the sword → q1 · the infancy in Msr → q2 (idea A) · the
  hunting ground → q3 (idea B) · the monastery → q4 (idea C) · the life story
  → q5 · the cast → q6 · before/after the duel → q7 · the duel's rules → q8
  (idea D) · was the fairness wise → q9 (Evaluate, idea D) · one episode to
  pass on → q10 (Create) · Kurkik Jalali → q11 · his death → q12 (idea D).

## 4. Proposed bank

**Three examples:**

1. *"Why did Sassoun have to pay tribute to the Caliph of Baghdad?"* → **"Why
   is the newborn David sent to Msra Melik's mother?"** A wrong fact replaced
   by the detail that makes the epic's conflict tragic rather than merely
   military: the enemy is the boy's half-brother, and they shared a nurse.
2. *"Which of David's qualities was most important in winning the duel?"* →
   **"How does the duel actually go?"** The old question had four virtues to
   choose between and no evidence; the new one requires knowing the agreement —
   three blows for Melik, one for David.
3. *"Why is David remembered as a hero?"* → **"He let his enemy strike first
   three times. Courage or recklessness?"** The first invites a recitation.
   The second is a real argument, and the grading brief accepts both verdicts.
