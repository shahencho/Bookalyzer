## 1. Book brief

- **Kind of book:** a real diary, written 12 June 1942 – 1 August 1944, later
  edited by its author into a book she meant to publish. Not a novel, and the
  bank should never treat it as one.
- **Readers in this app:** about 11–14. Younger than that and the ending is
  hard to hold.
- **Author's aim:** Anne's own aim changed mid-book — it begins as a private
  confidante and becomes, after March 1944, a deliberate record of the
  occupation written by someone who wanted to be a writer.
- **Big ideas:**
  - **A:** the Holocaust happened to individual people with ordinary days.
  - **B:** what hiding actually cost — silence, confinement, dependence on
    other people's courage.
  - **C:** Anne as a writer, not only as a victim; she revised her own work.
  - **D:** ordinary people chose to help at real risk.
- **What children connect with:** her arguments with her mother, boredom,
  wanting privacy, first love, wanting to be somebody.
- **Tone rule for this book:** honest, never graphic. Questions ask for
  understanding, not for details of the camps. The death is stated once, in
  the summary and one explanation, plainly and without description.

**Key scenes:** the diary as a birthday present · Margot's call-up · the move
behind the bookcase · the daily silence timetable · burglaries at night ·
Anne and her mother · Anne and Peter, 1944 · the Bolkestein broadcast and the
rewriting · the last entry · Miep saving the papers.

**Unsure / VERIFY:** none used. Dates, names, pseudonyms, the bookcase builder
and the survivor were verified against the Anne Frank House site and
Wikipedia. Peter's cat (Mouschi) is widely reported but I did not confirm it
in a primary source, so no question uses it.

## 3. Design for this book

- **Why this type mix:** a diary has no plot to order, so the ordering question
  runs on dates instead, which is exactly the skill this book needs (three
  weeks from birthday to hiding; the rewriting starting only five months before
  the end). Classification carries the timetable — the most concrete, least
  abstract way to convey what hiding meant. Two MCs are reserved for Anne's
  authorship (the pseudonyms; the broadcast). No crossword, no ranking: ranking
  suffering or people would be indefensible here.
- **Coverage map:** call-up → q1 · Kitty → q2 · bookcase → q3 · pseudonyms →
  q4 (idea C) · dates → q5 · four relationships → q6 · timetable → q7 (idea B) ·
  the rewriting → q8 (idea C) · "good at heart" → q9 (Evaluate) · what you'd
  miss → q10 (Create) · her ambition → q11 (idea C) · Miep and the papers →
  q12 (idea D).

## 4. Proposed bank

**Three examples:**

1. *"Why did Anne Frank and her family go into hiding in 1942?"* → **"Who
   received the call-up notice?"** The old question is answerable by anyone who
   has heard of the Holocaust. The new one needs the diary — and corrects the
   common assumption that the notice was for Otto, which Anne herself first
   assumed.
2. *"Which theme is most central: (b) hope, courage and the strength of the
   human spirit…"* → **"In March 1944 a minister asked people to keep their
   diaries. What did Anne do?"** A slogan question replaced by the fact that
   reframes the whole second half of the book: she was rewriting it as
   literature.
3. *classification "daily life in hiding" vs "the danger they faced"* →
   **"only during office hours" vs "only in the evenings or at weekends"**. The
   old split was vague and partly arbitrary (living cramped is both). The new
   one is a real, verifiable division that tells a child exactly what the
   experience was.
